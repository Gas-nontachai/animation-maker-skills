# Endpoint motion, joint motion and grip

Read this when a prompt includes curls, repetitions, held props, wrist orientation or drinking. Inspect the actual rig and runtime motion contract first. M3.1's optional motion.json `tracks` capability must be present; the M1 version range or the original M3 compileMotion export does not prove it. Older planners reject this field: report that capability gap rather than silently dropping authored motion.

## Select the operation from the intent

Use Reach when the requirement is a world-space hand destination: reaching to a rack, making first contact or returning a held prop. Reach may rotate both upper arm and forearm and only constrains its endpoint. Choose the bend branch from the intended elbow pose and inspect the intermediate angles. A correct hand endpoint does not certify a good shoulder/elbow pose.

For biceps curls, elbow flexion or a deliberate wrist tilt, use existing absolute local rotation keyframes. Keep the upper arm near a chosen resting angle after a short pickup-to-rest transition; vary forearm.rotation about the elbow. Use a modest rig-specific range, ease the up/down segments, allow a brief settle/hold, and repeat those keys. Do not label a sequence of hand targets as a Curl or use Reach for each repetition. Derive angles from the rig's neutral vectors and positive-Y-down convention; do not copy the Gym angles to another character. Do not introduce a dedicated Curl preset while existing tracks express the motion.

Put these keys in optional motion.json `tracks`, using existing M2 {object,part}, property: rotation and keyframes. Each authored track reserves its first-to-last interval, including holds, against conflicting generated properties. Keep its first value equal to the evaluated pose at its start (tolerance 1e-6); at time zero an explicit initial pose is allowed. Use at least two keys on frame boundaries, and preserve existing easing names. Part positions, pivots and scales remain rig-authored. Independent scene-object tracks can also be included, for example an explicitly requested following shadow. Never create prop position tracks to imitate a grip.

Split responsibilities by time/property: pickup → settle upper arm/forearm → curl rotations → return/putdown. Do not overlap Reach with authored rotations on the same arm joints. Dependencies express action prerequisites; explicit keyframe times control authored track intervals. Recompile the entire plan after joint/wrist edits so attachments include them. Editing the final character timeline after attachment baking leaves held objects on stale paths.

## Hand anchor and visible grip

The semantic hand is a transform/contact anchor; transparent-only hand assets are valid and must remain supported. Do not require fingers, a wrist role, a new rig version or a visible-hand capability check. A hand rotation track rotates about that part's pivot and drives its held object through attachment baking even if the SVG is invisible.

When authoring new visible geometry, choose the hand pivot deliberately: the planner places that pivot at the object's authored contact point. Draw a palm/thumb around it, overlap the forearm's endpoint, and inspect both joints and the grip at pickup, mid-curl, reversal, sip and release. A single hand pivot serving as wrist and grip is a stylized approximation; choose geometry and tilt that work with that limit. Keep upperArm → forearm → semantic hand direct when using the current Reach solver. Put visible palm geometry on the mapped hand without inserting an extra joint. If a supplied rig needs a separate wrist/grip hierarchy that the solver cannot support, disclose that limitation rather than bypassing validation.

Set the object's contact in its rig coordinates at the handle or grasped bottle body, not its arbitrary origin or its lip. Preserve the full hand/object offset with the compiler. Review layer grouping: an object above the character can hide the entire palm; a mathematically correct anchor is not evidence of a convincing grip. Choose prompt-appropriate geometry/layers in the editable source rather than changing renderer ordering. Preserve supplied rigs and original videos; create comparison copies for corrections.

## Drinking pose

Define two distinct points on the prop: grasp/contact and opening/lip. Define the mouth point in the character's actual geometry and transform it through existing world matrices. Aim the opening at the mouth; putting the hand near the face is insufficient. Choose a reachable hand destination and a modest local hand/wrist rotation so the attached prop's opening meets the mouth. Account for object scale, initial orientation, nonzero contact offset and ancestor rotations. For a rigid prop, openingWorld = objectWorld × openingLocal; compare that with mouthWorld at the sip hold. Use the runtime evaluator and existing matrix multiplication, not guessed local-position copying.

Use Reach for arm transport, an authored hand rotation for tilt, a brief hold, then reverse the tilt while returning the prop. Verify grip and lip alignment after compilation at every relevant rendered frame. Author an open bottle when appropriate; do not claim cap opening, liquid flow, swallowing or physical simulation. Report the gesture actually produced.

## Evidence and stopping conditions

Validate the project, render and inspect metadata, then decode frames around contact/reversal/release and at the sip pose. Check joint connectivity, shoulder stability for curls, contact offsets, wrist orientation and lip-to-mouth placement. A successful MP4 render or numeric endpoint check is not proof of natural-looking motion. Preserve the original and corrected SVG/rig/scene/plan/timeline/video paths and disclose limits. Regression, Manual E2E and Visual Acceptance belong to the owner.

## Phase 2: intent → constrained semantic poses

Use named poses when the prompt describes reusable joint configurations or repeated gestures, and constraints when a rig needs bounded/preferred articulation. Check the installed motion contract for `poses`, `constraints`, pose actions and optional secondary motion first; published M3 1.4.0 alone does not include these candidate capabilities. Existing authored tracks still work and remain the simplest choice for one-off keys.

The user describes the activity, not joint angles. Interpret “three controlled curls” as pickup → settle → alternate rest/flex poses three times → return. Inspect the authored link directions, pivots, scales and handedness; author rig-appropriate semantic targets and limits in the plan. A vertical human arm and a horizontal robot arm require different pose angles even with the same semantic roles. Do not copy yellow-shirt values into unrelated rigs. Keep the shoulder in both curl poses near the selected rest configuration; drive the forearm and use a suitable hand configuration. Pose duration/easing supplies the transition from the evaluated current pose; use dependencies and explicit frame-aligned starts. Add a short settling transition after pickup rather than jumping to the exercise pose.

Constraints are optional per object/semantic role: min/max bounds, optional preferred angle and optional stability (maximum departure from an interval's starting angle). Bounds must include the authored rig's initial pose. Preferred values never relax bounds. Constraints apply to presets, poses and authored rotation tracks; invalid or unsatisfiable inputs fail instead of being clamped into a different action. Without an explicit bend, a constrained Reach evaluates only the existing two elbow branches and picks a valid configuration closest to declared preferences (or the current pose). Keep an explicit bend only when the intended branch is deliberate; incompatible bends must produce a diagnostic.

A named pose has exact `joints` and optional `preferred` configuration targets for otherwise unspecified mapped joints. These are agent-authored degrees in local rig coordinates, not a renderer rig migration. Verify compatibility and constraints before compiling. For “drink”, keep the Phase 1 geometric grasp/opening/mouth calculation: Reach moves the arm while a nonconflicting named hand-tilt pose or authored rotation track tips the held object. Do not assume a generic sip pose can infer the opening or mouth from SVG artwork.

Secondary head/torso motion is disabled by default. Only add it when requested or useful and clearly describe it: set a low intensity, small bounded amplitude and an explicit interval away from the sip hold. Never animate an ancestor of a held hand or interfere with primary descendant-joint intervals; the compiler rejects those cases. A head nod can preserve the hand contact while still moving the mouth, so timing/visual review remains necessary. Keep primary motion and visible grip readable before adding supporting motion.

Preserve reference and Phase 1 projects, and create new Phase 2 comparisons. Compile and validate the complete plan, render, inspect metadata and decode key poses. Record both numerical evidence and what was actually viewed. Naturalness, Regression, Manual E2E and Visual Acceptance remain owner decisions.
